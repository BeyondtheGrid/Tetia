# Setting Reference — Leilon and the Sword Coast North

**Campaign:** Beyond the Dragon of Icespire Peak (Storm Lord's Wrath → Sleeping Dragon's Wake → Divine Contention)
**Rules:** D&D 2024
**Scope:** Player-facing only. This file holds what a well-informed resident of the region could plausibly know. It deliberately excludes the adventures' GM-side plot (villain identities, plans, twists). Anything learned in play goes in `campaign/log.md`, not here.
**Compiled:** 2026-10-04

How to use it:
- **GM facts override this file.** The GM's own lore posts are in `campaign/gm-lore.md`. If the GM's world differs from published lore, the GM wins; note the difference under "Table deviations" at the bottom.
- **Character knowledge is narrower than this file.** A character only knows what her background, home region, and travels would have taught her. Check `character/bible.md` knowledge boundaries before using any fact here in a post.

## 1. When we are

The adventures never state a year. The best evidence places them in the **early 1490s DR**:

- Neverember ordered Leilon reclaimed around 1487–1488 DR, and resettlement was nearing completion by about 1491 DR.
- The Essentials Kit (which the trilogy continues) is dated circa 1491 DR by the Forgotten Realms Wiki; one timeline analysis prefers 1492 or 1493 DR, and another argues the trilogy runs through 1490 DR.
- **Working assumption: 1491 DR, the Year of the Scarlet Witch.** Confirm with the GM; until then, avoid stating the year in posts.

### Recent history a Sword Coast native would know

| Year (DR) | Event | Why it matters to a local |
|---|---|---|
| 1385 | The Spellplague. Leilon's wizard tower loses its wards; its guardians rampage and the town is abandoned. | Leilon was a name travelers detoured around for a century. |
| 1451 | Mount Hotenow erupts, devastating Neverwinter and killing the ruling Alagondar line. | Anyone from Neverwinter has family stories of "the Ruining." |
| c. 1468 | Dagult Neverember arrives in ruined Neverwinter, names himself Lord Protector, and launches "New Neverwinter." | Rebuilding money, mercenaries, and a disputed claim to rule. |
| 1484–1487 | The Second Sundering. Mystra and the Weave return; the Spellplague's effects largely end. | Magic is "normal" again; old Spellplague scars and fears linger. |
| c. 1484–85 | Neverwinter's Chasm is sealed. | Neverwinter becomes livable again, at great cost. |
| c. 1487–88 | Neverember sends soldiers to reclaim Leilon. | The start of the town the party now defends. |
| 1489 | Neverember is ousted as Open Lord of Waterdeep; Laeral Silverhand replaces him. The Cult of the Dragon's bid to bring Tiamat to Faerûn is defeated. | Bad blood between Neverwinter and Waterdeep; dragon fears along the coast. |
| 1489–1491 | Giant troubles along the Sword Coast; elemental cults rise in the Dessarin Valley (1491). | General sense that the North is unstable and roads are dangerous. |
| Recent | A young white dragon terrorized Phandalin and the Sword Mountains before adventurers dealt with it. At this table the dragon was **Cryovain**, slain by the party at Icespire Hold (`log.md`). | Fresh local memory; the reason "dragon" is a loaded word around here. |

## 2. Leilon

**What it is.** A small former mining town on the High Road, roughly midway between Neverwinter and Waterdeep, being rebuilt by settlers from Neverwinter on Lord Neverember's coin.

**Where it is.** On the edge of a shallow mudflat running out to the Sea of Swords, under the crags of the Sword Mountains. The Mere of Dead Men lies just to the south.

**What it looks like now.** Mostly simple new wooden buildings among old stone ruins. Salvaged stones sometimes bear an old dwarven symbol: a star, a fish, and a mountain peak. The old High Tower of Thalivar at the center of town was torn down and is being rebuilt as the House of Thalivar.

**Who runs it.** A council loyal to Neverember:
- **Grizzelda Copperwraught:** in charge of rebuilding.
- **Sergeant Hazz Yorrum:** leads the garrison of Neverwinter soldiers.
- **Valdi Estapaar:** reviving the fishing industry.
- **Merrygold Brightshine:** halfling priest of Lathander, much loved, runs the town's main place of healing.
- Others who appear on the council at various points: Jack Torver (entrepreneur, runs the first general store) and Vangol Kuskolt (dwarven priest of Tyr, the town's judge).

**Economy.** Historically ore (copper, nickel, silver) from the eastern mines, sold to Waterdeep. Leilon has no true harbor: barges are poled out across the mudflats to meet ships, and the cranes can't work in high wind, so most goods come by caravan. Fishing is being revived.

**Places known to settlers.**
- **Lathander shrine:** first temple rebuilt; the town's healing and aid center.
- **Tymora shrine:** second temple; its caretaker also sells potions.
- **Tyr shrine:** third temple; doubles as the court.
- **Umber Hulk's Shell Inn:** first inn in the reclaimed town; run by the local gossip.
- **Torver's Post:** first general store.
- **The Fishery:** fish market, and the makeshift town hall in the evenings during rebuilding.
- **Quayside and the Barge Yard:** the barge harbor and its dwarven barge-master.
- **The mines:** east of town; old shafts reportedly open into the town itself.

**Local legends everyone hears within a tenday.**
- The ghosts of the **Swords of Leilon**, a 14th-century adventuring company killed in the Spellplague, are said to still guard the ruins.
- **Idol Island**, out in the marsh beyond Quayside, is covered in ancient ruins; locals hear whispers from it at night, and anyone who sleeps there supposedly has prophetic dreams.
- Gnome traders from a village in the western Sword Mountains historically visited monthly, said to be watched over by a gold dragon.
- Old rumors once tied Leilon to the Cult of the Dragon and the Zhentarim.

**The visible threat.** Followers of Talos have arrived in the region, and the weather has turned with them. (Everything beyond that is GM territory.)

## 3. Neverwinter and Lord Neverember

### Neverwinter
- Called the **Jewel of the North** and the **City of Skilled Hands**. Before the Ruining it was famed for gardens kept blooming through winter, multicolored glass lamps, precise water clocks, and fine jewelry.
- A warm, ice-free river and harbor, heated by fire elementals under Mount Hotenow.
- Residents are called **Neverwintans** (or Neveren). Traditional temperament: quiet, mannered, literate, efficient, respectful of deadlines and others' property. Since the Ruining: stubborn and determined.
- Old saying: **"By the clocks of Neverwinter"** — swearing something is true or precise (or complaining that someone is pedantic).
- Faith: Tyr was the city's god; his faith returned and became popular again after the Second Sundering. Torm, Selûne, Kelemvor, and others are also present. Asmodeus worship gained a foothold in the troubled decades.
- Population by the 1490s is cosmopolitan: humans, half-elves, some dwarves, tieflings commonly seen, dragonborn and Tarnian mercenaries, a few orcs and half-orcs.
- Even by 1491 DR, some outer walls and neighborhoods remained ruined and plagued by monsters and brigands.

### Dagult Neverember, Lord Protector
- Wealthy, charismatic, ambitious, proud, quick-tempered. Commonly seen as a capable leader who always looks for personal profit.
- Claims descent from Neverwinter's old royal line; the claim is widely doubted, and enemies call him the "Lord Pretender."
- Spent his own fortune rebuilding Neverwinter, paid Mintarn (Tarnian) mercenaries to protect it, and slowly won over most Neverwintans.
- Ousted as Open Lord of Waterdeep in 1489 DR and bitter about it. Afterward he grew more heavy-handed: high taxes on nobles and rich merchants, restrictions on guilds.
- He once banned spellscarred people from Neverwinter (the "Protector's Law," 1478 DR).
- Leilon's settlers and garrison owe loyalty to him personally; he still expects settlers to pay for his troops' protection of the High Road.

### How different people see him
| Who | Typical view |
|---|---|
| Returned Neverwintan refugees, laborers | Grateful; he brought work, food, and safety |
| Neverwintans who never left after the Ruining | Resentful of foreign mercenaries; slowly coming around |
| Neverwinter nobles and merchants | Squeezed by his taxes and guild limits |
| Waterdhavians | Suspicious; he was ousted amid scandal |
| Harpers | See him as imperial and tyrannical |
| Emerald Enclave, Neverwinter Wood elves | Relatively friendly; he backed sensible logging |
| Tarnians (Mintarn) | Feel abandoned after he cut back their contracts |

## 4. Waterdeep and Phandalin, briefly

- **Waterdeep:** the City of Splendors, the great power to the south on the High Road. Ruled by masked Lords and an Open Lord, Laeral Silverhand since 1489 DR. Tense relations with Neverember.
- **Phandalin:** a mining town on the Triboar Trail where it meets the High Road, in the Sword Mountain foothills. Recently troubled by a white dragon, since dealt with by adventurers. Often a supply source for Leilon.

## 5. Faiths that matter here

### Talos, the Storm Lord
- God of storms and destruction, pictured as a broad-shouldered, one-eyed bearded man. His clergy wear black robes shot with jagged gold and silver lines, and wear eye patches.
- Ordinary people fear rather than worship him. His priests are notorious for extorting sailors and farmers: pay, or Talos sends the storm.
- Worship is outlawed in many places, so shrines are usually secret.
- Leads the **Gods of Fury** with Auril (frost), Umberlee (sea), and Malar (the hunt).
- Hates gods of building, learning, nature, and healing light, including Chauntea, Lathander, Silvanus, Tyr, and Mystra.
- For settlers rebuilding a town by the sea, Talos is the nightmare god: storms wreck barges, cranes, and new timber.

### Other common faiths on the Sword Coast North
- **Lathander:** dawn, renewal, new beginnings. The obvious patron of a rebuilt town.
- **Tymora:** good luck; popular with adventurers. "Tymora's luck" is a common blessing.
- **Tyr:** justice; especially Neverwintan.
- **Umberlee:** the sea's cruelty; sailors and fisherfolk appease her.
- **Chauntea, Silvanus:** farmers and the wild; natural enemies of Talos's destruction.

## 7. Everyday life

**Calendar of Harptos.** Twelve months of 30 days, each made of three **tendays** (ten-day weeks). Days have no names; people say "the 15th of Hammer" or "Hammer 15th." Five festival days fall between months.

**Money.** Gold pieces are commonly called "dragons" on the Sword Coast (Waterdeep's coinage uses names like dragons, shards, and nibs; regional slang varies).

**Language.** Common is the trade tongue everywhere along the coast.
