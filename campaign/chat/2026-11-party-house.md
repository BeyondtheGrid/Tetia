Standing on the doorstep is a familiar face: Gallio Elibro. The wizard offers a polite smile and gives a slight nod. "Good morning! I hope I'm not interrupting anything. I realized after you left that I had neglected to settle your payment for clearing Thalivar's Tower."

Gallio reaches into his robes and produces a small leather pouch. He holds it out toward Kieran. "Five hundred gold pieces, as promised. The Order of the Many-Starred Cloak is grateful for your assistance. With the disturbances behind us, the workers can finally return to their duties."

He pauses at the doorway, looking past Kieran into the house. "Is everyone doing well? I hope your adventures have afforded you at least a little time to rest."

The morning settles into a comfortable rhythm as the party gathers around the kitchen table. Steam curls from the cups, the smell of fresh tea mingles with the sweetness of the pastries, and the leather pouch of gold rests in the middle of the table.

For a little while, the conversation turns to the events at the tower, the Watcher's gifts, and the strange journey that brought them all together. There is something reassuring about being home, surrounded by familiar faces, with no monsters lurking behind the next door.

But the morning doesn't remain peaceful for long.

A short while later, a folded piece of parchment slips beneath the front door. No knock accompanies it. Whoever delivered it has already disappeared down the street.

The parchment bears a simple wax seal: a stylized lighthouse surrounded by waves.

Inside is a brief message:

To the Adventurers Who Assisted in the Restoration of Thalivar's Tower,

Word of your recent work has reached those responsible for the safety and prosperity of Leilon. Your assistance is requested. There are matters along the coast that require capable hands, and we would appreciate an opportunity to discuss them with you.

Please make your way to the Leilon Town Hall at your earliest convenience.

— On behalf of the Council of Leilon

21. Tetia MercuryAPP — 11/14/26, 3:16 AM
At the first knock, the cup Tetia has been warming her hands on goes still halfway to her mouth, and she has counted three before she knows she is counting. She does not rise, for it is not her door to answer; her eyes go instead to the faces of those whose home this is, to learn from them whether a knock here is a thing to fear.

The ease she had found in the morning's quiet does not wholly return after that: not with Pip's doubts of the wizard's polish, and not with a letter that comes with no knock at all. A faint crease has settled between her brows, the softness has gone from her mouth, and her fingers lace together at the table's edge.

She rises to her full height, gathering back one long sleeve before it can trail through the teacups, and comes near enough to read the letter for herself, leaving it where it lies. She takes it in at a reader's pace, swift and exact, until one phrase calls her back.

Along the coast.

It was this coast's storms that sent her across the sea.
